# Founding Story

## The Hook

*The supply chain world is drowning in data but starving for practitioner intelligence.*

---

## Where This Started

Six months into an international MBA at IE Business School in Madrid, I found myself sitting in rooms with supply chain professionals from 40+ countries — people with experience in FMCG operations in Lagos, procurement in Riyadh, logistics in Seoul, manufacturing SC in Bangalore, and 3PL management in Rotterdam.

None of that knowledge was being captured anywhere.

Every conversation happened once and disappeared. There was no structure, no format, no place for it to compound into something useful.

## What I Noticed

The existing supply chain communities I could find fell into three categories:
- Broad and noisy — too many topics, too little depth
- Formal and credential-focused — useful for certifications, not for real conversations
- Passive — LinkedIn groups full of reposts with no original thinking

None of them felt like a place where a practitioner with a real logistics challenge could bring it to a table and walk away with something useful.

## The Decision

I decided to build what was missing: a practitioner-first space where real supply chain problems get diagnosed, where sessions produce written outputs, and where membership means active participation — not passive consumption.

I chose Madrid and Mumbai as the two founding chapters because they are the two cities I know, and because they sit on one of the most consequential supply chain corridors in the world: the India-Europe trade axis accelerating through China+1, PLI schemes, and EU-India trade negotiations.

## The Role: Is this a real job?
When I tell people I am building a "Supply Chain Community," they often ask: *"Is that a real job?"*

The answer is yes—if you look at the right industries. In the software world, companies like **Y Combinator** and **OpenAI** have proven that "Community Builder" is a high-stakes, high-impact operations role. They hire strategic operators to bridge the gap between technical products and practitioners. These roles in the US command salaries from **$90K to $160K**.

**Project Antigravity** is the first attempt to bring this level of strategic community building to the "physical" world of supply chain. We are proving that there is a massive career opportunity at the intersection of **operations, community, and data**.

## What This Is Not

This is not a LinkedIn group.  
This is not a certification body.  
This is not a consulting club.  
This is not a vanity project.

It is a working community — small at first, built on rigour, designed to grow only as fast as it can maintain quality.

## The Founding Principle

*Most supply chain communities share articles. We solve problems.*
