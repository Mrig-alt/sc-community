# SC Frameworks Reference

A living reference of supply chain frameworks used and discussed in community sessions.

---

## 1. Kraljic Matrix

**What it is**: A 2x2 framework for segmenting procurement items by supply risk and profit impact. Developed by Peter Kraljic in 1983.

**The four quadrants**:
- Strategic (high risk, high impact) — manage relationships, dual-source, long-term contracts
- Leverage (low risk, high impact) — exploit buying power, competitive tendering
- Bottleneck (high risk, low impact) — secure supply, hold safety stock, find alternatives
- Non-critical (low risk, low impact) — automate, simplify, reduce transaction cost

**When to use it**: Supplier segmentation, procurement strategy, supply risk assessment.

**Common mistake**: Treating all suppliers the same. The matrix forces differentiation.

---

## 2. SCOR Model (Supply Chain Operations Reference)

**What it is**: A cross-industry standard framework for SC performance management. Covers five processes: Plan, Source, Make, Deliver, Return.

**When to use it**: SC benchmarking, process mapping, identifying gaps across the end-to-end chain.

**Common mistake**: Using it as a reporting template instead of a diagnostic tool.

---

## 3. S&OP — Sales and Operations Planning

**What it is**: A monthly business management process that aligns demand, supply, and financial plans. Bridges commercial and operational functions.

**Maturity levels**:
1. Reactive — firefighting, no process
2. Anticipatory — spreadsheet-based, disconnected
3. Integrated — cross-functional, single agreed plan
4. Orchestrated — external signals included, scenario planning

**When to use it**: Diagnosing misalignment between sales forecasts and operational capacity.

---

## 4. The Bullwhip Effect

**What it is**: Demand variability amplification as you move upstream in the supply chain. A 5% demand change at retail can become a 40% swing at the manufacturer.

**Causes**: Demand forecast errors, batch ordering, price fluctuations, shortage gaming.

**Fix**: Information sharing, vendor-managed inventory, smaller more frequent orders.

---

## 5. Theory of Constraints (TOC)

**What it is**: A management philosophy that any SC is limited by its weakest constraint. Improving non-constraints does not improve throughput.

**The five steps**: Identify the constraint, exploit it, subordinate everything else, elevate it, repeat.

**When to use it**: Throughput bottleneck analysis, production scheduling, logistics flow.

---

## 6. Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)

**What it is**: Full cost analysis beyond purchase price — including quality, logistics, risk, compliance, lead time, and switching costs.

**When to use it**: Supplier selection, make-vs-buy decisions, nearshoring analysis.

---

## 7. MECE Applied to SC Problem-Solving

**What it is**: Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive — a thinking structure from consulting used to diagnose SC problems without gaps or overlaps.

**How to apply**: Break any SC challenge into non-overlapping sub-problems that together cover the whole issue.

**Example**: Diagnosing a delivery delay — separate into demand-side, supply-side, and logistics-side causes before diagnosing each.

---

## 8. Porter's Value Chain for SC

**What it is**: A framework for mapping where value is created and cost is incurred across a firm's activities, including inbound/outbound logistics, operations, and procurement.

**When to use it**: Identifying where SC decisions create or destroy competitive advantage.
